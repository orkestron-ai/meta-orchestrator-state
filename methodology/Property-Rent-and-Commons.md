> A régime must answer: **what can be owned, and who owns the value nobody created?**
> Axiacracy's answer — **you own what you create (earned value); you do not own what nature
> or society created (unearned rent).** This is Georgism generalized from land to *all* rent
> — including the decisive modern case, **AI-compute/data as the new "land."**

## 1. The principle

- **Earned value → private.** Value a transformer actually *created* (its realized
  contribution) is its own.
- **Unearned rent → commons.** Value an actor *captured without creating* — produced by
  nature, society, position, or concentration — belongs to all.

This is a **third position**: *not capitalism* (which lets rent be captured privately),
*not socialism* (which collectivizes even earned value and the means of production).

## 2. What can and cannot be owned

| Thing | Ownership |
|---|---|
| **Æ / standing / reputation** | **Not ownable/transferable** — an accounting record of what you realized, not an asset (a coin) |
| **Produced goods** (your yogurt, tools, home-as-dwelling) | **Private** — you produced or fairly acquired them |
| **The commons** (land, natural resources, spectrum, the knowledge commons, **AI-compute/data/network-effects**) | **Not privately ownable** — they yield **rent**, which is commons-owed |
| **Capital / means of production** | **Private *up to* the value you created; the rent it extracts beyond your contribution is commons-owed** (bounded ownership) |

Organizing rule: **ownership extends exactly as far as creation, and no further.**

## 3. Earned vs unearned rent — measurable in the ledger (VMT)

Georgism observed that land value = *natural qualities + community activity*, **not the
owner's effort** → unearned. Generalize via VMT:

- **Earned value** = Æ the actor **created** (consumed value, transformed it, produced new
  value that was realized).
- **Unearned rent** = Æ **captured in excess of Æ created** — from **nature** (land,
  resources), **society** (network effects, location value, public infrastructure, the
  research commons), **position/luck** (chokepoints, first-mover), or **concentration**
  (monopoly rent).

In VMT this excess-capture is exactly **parasitism / leakage (T11)**: realized value
received persistently exceeds value created. Therefore **rent is a measurable quantity in
the Æ-ledger**: `rent = Æ_captured − Æ_created`. Real economies struggle to see rent; a
value-flow ledger can, in principle, *compute* it.

## 4. Rent enforcement reuses the existing overlay — no new machinery

Capturing unearned rent is a form of **concentration / externality anti-value** — so it is
priced by the machinery already built (`Value-Money-Coupling.md`, `Markets-and-Civilizational-Value.md`):

- the **concentration charge** (economic ceiling) and **externality overlay** price captured rent;
- **clawback** recovers concealed/gross historic rent;
- the recovered rent is **routed to the commons** — a **citizen dividend** and/or
  **civilizational reinvestment** — via the same `remediation_routing`.

So the property regime adds a *principle and an accounting*, not a new enforcement engine.

## 5. AI-compute / data as the new "land" — the anti-BigTech core

Henry George: land rent belongs to all because land value comes from nature + community, not
the owner. **The exact parallel in the AI economy:**

- **Foundation models, compute, data, and network effects** derive their value largely from
  **humanity's collective data** (models trained on everyone's output), **public science**
  (the research commons), and **network effects** (value from universal participation) —
  **not solely from the owner's effort.**
- BigTech captures this as **private rent → concentration** — the article's central
  anti-value.
- **Axiacracy's answer:** the **unearned portion of AI-infrastructure rent is commons-owed**,
  routed to a **citizen dividend + civilizational reinvestment** (education, cognitive
  development, scientific ecosystems, remediation of displacement). This **mechanizes the
  article's "mandatory contributions"** through a Georgist lens.
- **Data as a commons:** an individual's data has value mostly through **aggregation**
  (network effect); the rent from data is partly collective → grounds a **data dividend**.

This is the sharpest single application of the whole methodology: **AI rent → commons.**

## 6. Land & housing

Land is **held under a use-right (usufruct), not freehold**: you may possess and use it, but
its **location rent** (created by the community, not by you) is **commons-owed** (a
value-ledger analogue of the Georgist land-value tax). This defuses **housing-as-speculation**
— a prime channel of the value concentration the article warns of.

## 6a. Renting, leasing & inheritance — earned service vs unearned rent *(clarification)*

**Not all rental income is unearned rent.** The split applies to the income **decomposed**,
not to "renting" as an activity.

- **Renting out a produced asset** (a car, tools, equipment, a building's **structure**) is
  **earned service income**: you provide real use-value (mobility, capability), bear
  depreciation, maintenance and risk, and tie up earned capital. Income ≈ value created →
  **earned.** A car is not land; it carries no community-created location rent.
- **Real estate splits in two** (classic Georgism taxes *land*, never *improvements*): the
  **structure** you built/maintain and the **service of providing it** are **earned**; only
  the **land/location** component — value the community created, not you — is **unearned →
  commons-owed.** You may build, hold and rent a building; you owe commons-rent only on the
  land under it.
- **The measure discriminates automatically.** `unearned = captured − created`. A
  competitively priced rental: captured ≈ created → **no unearned rent**. A **scarcity/monopoly**
  rental (you control all the cars in town and gouge): the **excess above competitive** is
  unearned → charged. We never hand-classify "rentals bad" — the ledger measures the excess.
- **Inheritance.** Produced assets are private and **bequeathable** — a gift of the owner's
  earned value; you may inherit a house or a business you cannot use yourself. Three limits stop
  it becoming dynastic power: (i) the **land/location rent stays commons-owed** whoever holds
  title; (ii) the **concentration ceiling** still applies — a modest inheritance passes freely, a
  dynastic mega-estate triggers concentration charges; (iii) **Æ/standing is non-transferable**
  — you inherit *goods*, never *civic power/reputation*. "You can't live in it yourself" is fine:
  you may **hold, use, rent** (earning on the structure, owing on the land), **or transfer** it —
  nothing forces occupancy.

## 7. Capital, investment & credit when money is demoted *(the hard part — most speculative)*

If Æ is non-transferable and money is only a settlement rail, how is **long-horizon value
creation financed**? Axiacracy's answer, from what is already built:

- **Strategic demand *is* the long-horizon investment function.** The state funds the future
  by issuing **Æ-credits/incentives** for future-oriented contribution (research,
  infrastructure, demographic) — **Civilizational Missions (D1) + Incentives (A7)** — driven
  by civilizational need, not private return.
- **The commons fund.** Unearned rent (§4–5) pools into a **sovereign rent fund** — the
  society-level investment pool allocated by strategic demand (a rent-funded sovereign
  wealth fund + citizen dividend).
- **Earned entitlement as "capital."** A high contributor's **draw-rights** (access to
  scarce resources) can be directed into a venture — investment whose "capital" is *earned
  access*, not transferable money.
- **Credit = resources advanced against future *realized* value.** A venture receives
  resources now against a **contract for committed future Æ-delta**, attested when realized
  (reuses orkestro.net **BR14: settlement decoupled from value**; escrow/clawback handles
  default → standing debited). Money may still be *lent* at the convenience layer, but the
  sovereign accounting is "resources advanced against future realized Æ."
- **Returns to creation, not to ownership.** You may profit from **creating** value; you may
  **not** earn a pure rentier return on **owning** rent-extracting capital — that return *is*
  unearned rent (§3), commons-owed. **Interest** (pure time-rent on money) is itself a form
  of unearned rent and is correspondingly **constrained**.

**Honest flag:** this is the most novel and least-tested part of the doctrine; it needs its
own stress-test (how ventures with no near-term realized value get financed; risk-bearing;
avoiding a state-monopoly-on-capital failure mode). Marked as a research priority.

## 8. Positioning
**Private ownership of the earned; commons ownership of the unearned/rent.** Georgism's
"neither socialism nor laissez-faire" — **generalized from land to all rent (incl. AI/data)
and made measurable via the value-flow ledger.**
