# Systemic-Harm Adjudication — when the "event" is a pattern and the victim is a cohort

> Edge harm (a spoiled yogurt) is easy: one flow, one culprit, a named victim. **Systemic harm**
> (content dependency, concentration, epistemic erosion) is the hard case: the "event" is a
> **pattern over many flows**, the victim is a **cohort**, causation is **statistical**, the culprits
> are **many**, and by the time proof arrives the damage may be **irreversible**. This document
> handles that case without abandoning due process.

## 1. Pattern-as-event
The trigger is not a single transaction but a **measured shift in a cohort's axis beyond a baseline**
— e.g., a youth cohort's cognitive axis declining faster than a counterfactual baseline, attributable
to a *class* of flows (attention-capturing platforms). The **cohort-level anti-value crossing a
threshold** is the "event" that enters adjudication.

## 2. Statistical (population-attributable) causation
You cannot prove *this* video harmed *this* person. So — exactly as in tobacco/opioid/climate
litigation — the standard is **population-attributable causation:** establish that the *class of
flows* produced an *excess* of harm in the cohort **beyond a counterfactual baseline** (the
attributable fraction), not individual causation. Burden of proof sits on the **excess-over-baseline**,
adjudicated with the accused class's right to contest confounders.

## 3. Apportionment across many culprits
Systemic harm is co-produced. Responsibility is **apportioned by contribution** (proportional exposure
— e.g., each platform's share of captured attention-hours — with a **concealment multiplier**; Shapley
only for small, well-defined sets). Each actor can **contest its share** (my design wasn't the harmful
one; my share is smaller; causation is confounded).

## 4. Class-wide, statistical restitution
Because individual harm can't be precisely attributed, restitution is **routed to the affected cohort
and the damaged dimension**, not to named individuals: the apportioned responsibility shares **fund the
remediation** of the harmed axis for the harmed cohort (cognitive-health programs for the youth cohort,
paid by the attention-capturers in proportion to their share). This is remediation routing at cohort
scale. Restorative-first still holds — the aim is to **rebalance the damaged dimension**, plus a
deterrent scaled by magnitude × intent × recurrence (the exponential escalator).

## 5. Precaution vs. proof — the irreversibility problem
The critics' sharpest point: long-lag harm may cross **irreversible** thresholds before it can be
proven. Resolution, consistent with confidence-graded coercion:
- **Act earlier, on lower confidence, with *soft* precautionary measures** — illuminate the trend,
  shift incentives, and **hold escrow against the suspected harm** (escrow sized to the detection lag).
  If the harm materializes, restitution is pre-funded; if it doesn't, escrow releases.
- **Escalate to coercion only as confidence rises.** So confidence gates the *hardness* of the
  response over time: precaution (soft, low-confidence) → charge (higher) → full remediation (proven).
- This is a **bounded precautionary principle** — early soft action specifically for **irreversible**
  classes, never full coercion on weak evidence.

## 6. Who brings the case
The relevant **Sub-Agent** (e.g., Cognitive-Health) or the **Equity/Ombudsman** guardian brings the
systemic action **on behalf of the cohort** — like a public-nuisance suit by a state attorney general —
so individual victims need not litigate. A **citizen initiative** can also trigger it. The accused
class gets full due process; judged by the **thresholds/norms in force at the time** (non-retroactivity)
— except concealment, which was wrong then too (perpetual liability).

## 7. Honest limit
Systemic causation is **genuinely hard and contestable** — this is where **adversarial measurement**
(multi-source, prediction markets, anomaly detection), **retrospective revaluation**, and
**confidence-grading** matter most, and where the **simulation is most needed** to test whether the
estimator is robust or gameable (a platform maintaining a short-term high-value facade to dodge
long-lag revaluation is a first-class thing to red-team in MOS). The doctrine does not claim systemic
harm is cleanly measurable; it claims a *disciplined, contestable, precaution-bounded* procedure for it.
